# A Survey of World Models in Artificial Intelligence and Reinforcement Learning

## TL;DR
- World models enable intelligent agents to learn internal representations of environment dynamics, significantly boosting sample efficiency in reinforcement learning [1][2].
- Modern architectures advance from compact recurrent state-space models (RSSM) and discrete token transformers (IRIS) to continuous diffusion models (DIAMOND) and large-scale interactive video engines (Genie) [3][4][5][6].
- Scaling generative world models for robotics and autonomous driving unlocks powerful simulation and zero-shot control, though long-horizon rollouts remain challenged by compounding error and physical inconsistency [7][8][9].

## Background
World models refer to computational systems that learn an internal representation and transition model of their environment's dynamics, allowing agents to predict future states, rewards, and observations given past actions [1][10]. Rooted in classical state-space models, predictive coding, and Schmidhuber's early work on "Learning to Think", modern world models decouple representation learning from policy optimization [1][10]. By constructing an environment simulator in latent or observation space, agents can perform model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) and tree search entirely within imagination rollouts, circumventing the massive sample complexity of pure model-free trial-and-error [1][2][11].

## Foundations and Classical Architectures
The modern paradigm of reinforcement learning with world models was catalyzed by Ha & Schmidhuber (2018), who introduced a neural world model comprising a spatial compression network (V model) and a recurrent temporal prediction model (M model) trained via evolution [1][10]. Building upon this foundation, Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) formalizes world models through core components: representation models, transition models, and reward/value predictors [3][2]. PlaNet introduced the Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM), dividing latent states into deterministic recurrent sequences and stochastic components to capture multiple futures over extended horizons [2]. Subsequent iterations like Dreamer refined RSSM with categorical latent variables, KL balancing, and symlog predictions, achieving high sample efficiency across more than 150 diverse tasks and setting benchmarks in complex environments like Minecraft [3]. Simultaneously, tree-based planning algorithms such as MuZero demonstrated that iterative dynamics models predicting policy, value, and reward without knowing environment rules could achieve state-of-the-art performance across Atari and classical board games [11].

## Latent Dynamics and Discrete Token Approaches
Transitioning beyond continuous Gaussian latent models, recent paradigms explore discrete tokenization and autoregressive modeling of environment dynamics [3][4]. IRIS demonstrated that world models can be formulated as sequence modeling problems by pairing a discrete autoencoder with a GPT-like autoregressive Transformer backbone, achieving a mean human-normalized score of 1.046 on the Atari 100k benchmark after only 100,000 steps of real-time experience [4]. These latent dynamics models operate entirely in imagination, training actor-critic controllers solely on synthetic rollout trajectories [3][4]. Furthermore, probing studies into latent representations reveal that both discrete token transformers and continuous models develop linearly decodable representations of critical game variables like object positions and scores [12]. However, maintaining long-horizon temporal consistency without latent collapse requires rigorous regularization, such as KL balancing, free bits, and categorical mixture regularizers [3].

## Generative Video World Models and Neural Game Engines
The scaling of generative models has expanded world models from compact low-dimensional representations to high-fidelity video simulation and interactive environments [13][5][6]. Genie (11B parameters) pioneered unsupervised foundation world models trained from unlabelled video data using a spatiotemporal video tokenizer (VQ-VAE with ST-ViViT), a MaskGIT-based autoregressive dynamics model, and a causal latent action model that learns continuous action spaces between frames without ground-truth labels [5]. In parallel, continuous diffusion world models like DIAMOND (DIffusion As a Model Of eNvironment Dreams) bypass discrete token compression by utilizing EDM parameterization, achieving a human-normalized score of 1.46 on Atari 100k and functioning as interactive neural game engines trained on static gameplay like Counter-Strike: Global Offensive [6]. In robotics and embodied AI, geometry-grounded video world models such as GEM-4D inject dense 4D correspondence supervision to ensure that generated future videos maintain the physical consistency required for robot manipulation [14].

## Trends and Open Problems
Recent developments highlight a sharp divergence between proprietary text-conditioned video models and domain-specific action-conditioned world models [15]. While OpenAI's Sora provides emergent general physics simulation and minute-long video generation, it lacks direct action-conditioning interfaces necessary for closed-loop control, whereas domain-specific models like GAIA-1 utilize video-language-action architectures with fine-grained action conditioning [15]. In autonomous driving, driving world models (DWM) face severe challenges in long-horizon prediction, where compounding errors lead to blurriness, semantic degradation, and out-of-distribution artifacts like lateral sliding or premature stopping [9][16]. Recent innovations such as Orbis mitigate these issues using continuous autoregressive flow matching over 280 hours of driving data [9]. Key open problems include bridging the gap between perception and action alignment, scaling diffusion policy optimization [17], ensuring formal safety guarantees [16], and overcoming memory and computational constraints in dense geometric voxel modeling [16].
